## Executive summary

Hiring for AI and data roles grew 80.2% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, and the growth is concentrated in AI engineering and data infrastructure rather than in traditional analytics roles. This paper synthesizes DataCamp's _State of AI Careers 2026_ findings with its published role guides to describe the skills, knowledge and professional competencies each role requires.

The main findings:

- **A universal base.** Communication, Python, SQL and computer science fundamentals appear in all 25 roles DataCamp tracks ([DataCamp press release, August 2026](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/)).
- **AI competence is now near-universal.** AI skills are a core requirement in 22 of the 25 roles, and DataCamp reports a 56% wage premium for AI skills.
- **Growth is uneven.** AI engineer postings rose 255% and generative AI engineer postings 197%. Data engineer, Python developer, data architect and machine learning engineer roles also grew strongly, while data analyst, data scientist and business intelligence roles each grew less than 50%.
- **Roles separate by position in the pipeline.** Some roles build and govern the data, some turn it into decisions, some model it, and some ship AI products. The skill profiles differ mainly in what sits on top of the shared base.
- **Human skills are rising in value.** As AI automates mechanical analysis and reporting, employers weight explanation, judgment and business context more heavily.

Coverage note: DataCamp's full 25-role breakdown is available only in its gated report and interactive tool, whose role selector lists about 210 job titles that lead to the main roles. This document profiles the 14 roles that are confirmed to be in that set and supplements them with 14 adjacent roles from DataCamp's career guides. Later sections cover career paths, certifications, how employers assess candidates, compensation and a glossary; the final section lists what is verified and what remains open.

Sources: [DataCamp, The State of AI Careers 2026](https://www.datacamp.com/resources/whitepapers/state-of-ai-careers-2026); [Business Wire release via 01net](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/08/19/the-ai-job-that-surged-255-in-2026-and-pays-113000/)

## Market context

DataCamp analyzed two million deduplicated job postings across 85 regions from January 2024 to April 2026, using Lightcast labor-market data, alongside research from the World Economic Forum, the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Stanford, Anthropic and PwC. After a slight decline in 2024 and stagnation in 2025, AI and data postings rebounded sharply.

**Where growth is concentrated**

- AI engineering postings grew 255% year over year, the highest rate of any tracked role. Generative AI engineering grew 197%. Both had median base salaries above $100,000 globally.
- Infrastructure-oriented roles (data engineer, Python developer, data architect, machine learning engineer) also grew strongly.
- Traditional data analyst, data scientist and business intelligence roles each grew less than 50%.
- Data science managers had the highest median base salary in the analysis, approaching $190,000.

**What is changing inside the roles**

DataCamp's headline conclusion is that jobs are not disappearing; the tasks inside them are changing. Employers increasingly look for people who can use AI and data tools and also explain insights, apply judgment and connect technical work to business decisions. The report names prompt engineering, agent management and domain-specific applications as AI capabilities that complement the fundamentals.

**The early-career shift**

Employment for workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed fields such as customer support and accounting fell 6% between late 2022 and September 2025, while older workers in the same fields saw employment rise 6% to 9%. DataCamp reports this as a 16% relative employment decline for entry-level workers. DataCamp found no systematic unemployment spike among the most AI-exposed workers, so exposure to AI does not translate directly into job replacement. The report cites the World Economic Forum's projection of 170 million new roles and 92 million displaced roles globally by 2030, a net gain of 78 million.

Sources: [DataCamp, The State of AI Careers 2026](https://www.datacamp.com/resources/whitepapers/state-of-ai-careers-2026); [Business Wire release via 01net](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/)

## Competencies required across every role

Four competencies are reported for all 25 roles, and a fifth, AI skills, for 22 of them. Together they form the baseline that DataCamp's analysis treats as non-negotiable.

- **Communication.** Translating technical work into decisions for non-technical stakeholders, and explaining method and limitations. Employers want practitioners who can walk through a process and connect findings to business outcomes, not only produce outputs.
- **Python.** The common language for data preparation, pipelines, modeling and AI application development.
- **SQL.** The standard interface to relational and warehouse data, used from analyst to AI engineer roles.
- **Computer science fundamentals.** Program structure, data structures, algorithms and system behavior. This is what separates a maintainable data product from a one-off script.
- **AI skills (22 of 25 roles).** Working fluency with generative AI: prompt and context design, evaluating model output, managing agents, and applying models to domain problems. DataCamp reports that AI skills command a 56% wage premium.

**Second-tier competencies**

Across DataCamp's role guides, a second layer recurs in most technical roles, although the published data does not report it as universal: cloud platforms (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), statistics and probability, machine learning fundamentals, version control with Git, and data cleaning and quality practices. Roles at the infrastructure end of the pipeline add orchestration, containers and CI/CD; roles at the analytics end add visualization and business-domain knowledge.

Sources: [Business Wire release via 01net](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/); [DataCamp, The State of AI Careers 2026](https://www.datacamp.com/resources/whitepapers/state-of-ai-careers-2026); second-tier observations are drawn from the role guides cited under each profile below.

## Role profiles

These 14 roles are confirmed to be in DataCamp's 25-role set, from its tool page, press release and published coverage. Under each role, skills are listed in the order DataCamp's own guides present them, most central first. An evidence note states how well sourced each profile is, so thin profiles are not mistaken for strong ones.

### Group A: Data foundation and governance

#### Data Engineer

**Evidence:** strong (two DataCamp guides).

**Mandate:** Designs, builds and maintains the systems that collect, store, transform and deliver data at scale. DataCamp notes that most of the time goes to maintaining and improving existing pipelines, and that in 2026 the role increasingly supports AI work such as feature stores, training-data pipelines and LLM data infrastructure.

**Core skills, in order:** Python; SQL; data modeling and databases (relational, NoSQL, warehouses, lakes); ETL/ELT and orchestration with Airflow and dbt; cloud platforms; distributed computing with Spark; stream processing with Kafka or Flink; shell scripting; data quality and observability; containers and orchestration (Docker, Kubernetes). Java or Scala is needed mainly for heavy Spark work.

**Knowledge:** dimensional modeling (star and snowflake schemas), medallion architecture, batch versus streaming, data governance and data contracts.

**Human skills:** problem-solving, collaboration with analysts and scientists, communication with business leaders.

Sources: [How to Learn Data Engineering](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-learn-data-engineering); [How to Become a Data Engineer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-data-engineer)

#### Data Architect

**Evidence:** thin. DataCamp gives no ranked skill list for this role.

**Mandate:** Designs enterprise-wide data systems, governance standards and integration patterns. DataCamp presents it as a specialization path from data engineering, and its modern data architecture course covers ingestion, serving, governance and orchestration.

**Indicative skills:** Outside DataCamp, hiring guides list data warehouse design, data modeling, SQL, ETL architecture, cloud data platforms and data governance. Treat this as indicative, not as DataCamp's ranking.

Sources: [How to Become a Data Engineer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-data-engineer); [Understanding Modern Data Architecture](https://www.datacamp.com/courses/understanding-modern-data-architecture); [Interview Kickstart, Data Architect skills](https://interviewkickstart.com/skills/data-architect)

#### Chief Data Officer

**Evidence:** thin. DataCamp has no skills guide for this role; the description below comes from outside sources.

**Mandate:** A senior executive responsible for data strategy, data quality, governance and security, and for turning data into business value.

**Competencies cited:** data analytics and data management, governance and security, leadership, and presentation skills.

Sources: [Splunk, Chief Data Officer responsibilities](https://www.splunk.com/en_us/blog/learn/chief-data-officer-role-responsibilities); [QA, What is a Chief Data Officer](https://www.qa.com/resources/blog/what-is-a-chief-data-officer/)

### Group B: Analytics and business intelligence

#### Data Analyst

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide and certification).

**Mandate:** Gathers, processes and analyzes data to answer business questions, and explains the findings to decision-makers.

**Core skills, in order:** programming in Python, R and SQL; visualization in Tableau or Power BI; statistical analysis (descriptive, inferential, hypothesis testing); data wrangling and cleaning. Advanced: machine learning and big data tools. DataCamp's certification tests data management, exploratory analysis, statistical experimentation and data communication.

**Human skills:** communication and data storytelling, problem-solving, attention to detail. DataCamp also stresses domain knowledge, such as medical terminology in healthcare or financial metrics in finance.

Sources: [9 Essential Data Analyst Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/data-analyst-skills-for-career-success); [DataCamp certification](https://www.datacamp.com/certification)

#### Business Intelligence Analyst

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide, dated December 2023).

**Mandate:** Turns complex data into reports and dashboards that support executive decisions. BI work is mostly descriptive analytics.

**Core skills, in order:** SQL; visualization in Power BI or Tableau; statistical analysis; data extraction and exploration; data cleaning and preprocessing; predictive analytics. Python or R is useful but optional.

**Human skills:** communication, problem-solving, attention to detail, teamwork, and knowledge of the business domain.

Source: [How to Become a BI Analyst](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-business-intelligence-analyst)

#### BI Developer / Architect

**Evidence:** moderate for the developer half (DataCamp's Power BI developer guide); no separate source for the architect half.

**Core skills, in order:** data modeling (relationships, cardinality, star schema); DAX calculations; data transformation with Power Query; visualization and report design; Excel and SQL. Optional: Python or R scripts for predictive analytics. The Microsoft PL-300 certification is the recognized credential.

**Career ladder:** junior Power BI developer or data analyst, then Power BI developer or BI analyst, then senior developer, consultant or BI manager.

**Human skills:** business context, communicating insights, attention to detail, and data privacy and security.

Source: [An 8-Step Guide to Becoming a Power BI Developer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/becoming-a-power-bi-developer)

#### Analytics Product Manager

**Evidence:** none. No DataCamp or other skills source was found for this title. The nearest documented profile is the AI Product Manager under Adjacent roles.

### Group C: Modeling and data science

#### Data Scientist

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide updated May 2026).

**Mandate:** Frames the question, builds the analysis or model, and judges whether the result can be trusted. DataCamp argues that as AI assistants write boilerplate code and first-draft queries, the role's value is shifting toward problem framing, statistical judgment and evaluation of model output.

**Core skills, in order:** Python and R; statistics and math; SQL and NoSQL; data visualization; machine learning; deep learning; natural language processing; generative AI and LLMs (prompt design, retrieval-augmented generation, evaluation); AI-assisted development; big data; cloud computing; MLOps fundamentals.

**Human skills:** communication, storytelling, critical thinking, business acumen, problem-solving, teamwork.

Source: [How to Become a Data Scientist](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-data-scientist)

#### Data Science Manager

**Evidence:** compensation and growth data only. DataCamp publishes no skills list for this role.

**Known:** This role had the highest median base salary in DataCamp's analysis, approaching $190,000, and postings grew roughly 20% year over year.

Sources: [Forbes on DataCamp's report](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/08/19/the-ai-job-that-surged-255-in-2026-and-pays-113000/); [Business Wire release via 01net](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/)

#### Machine Learning Engineer

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide, dated December 2023, plus the ML jobs guide).

**Mandate:** Sits between data science and software engineering: takes a model from experiment to a scalable product and monitors it in production. DataCamp notes the role has surpassed data science as one of the fastest-growing jobs in the US.

**Core skills, in order:** statistics and probability; machine learning algorithms; model evaluation and monitoring in production; DevOps and CI/CD; cloud platforms; version control; programming in Python, then Java or C++, with object-oriented design.

**Human skills:** communication, problem-solving, continuous learning.

Sources: [Top 12 Machine Learning Engineer Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/machine-learning-engineer-skills); [The Best Machine Learning Jobs](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/top-machine-learning-jobs-in-2022)

### Group D: AI product engineering

#### AI Engineer

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide updated June 2026, plus Forbes).

**Mandate:** Designs, builds and maintains AI-based systems, bridging data science and production software. This is the fastest-growing role in DataCamp's data.

**Core skills, in order:** programming (Python first, then Java, R, C++, JavaScript); data modeling and engineering, including vector databases; big data analysis; machine learning models and evaluation; cloud AI services; deployment and DevOps with Docker and Kubernetes; MLOps; AI security and privacy; prompt engineering. Advanced: linear algebra, calculus and statistics; neural network architectures; generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation and agent frameworks. Forbes' summary of the report adds responsible AI and cloud deployment.

**Human skills:** communication and collaboration, adaptability, critical thinking, domain knowledge.

Sources: [16 Essential AI Engineer Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/essential-ai-engineer-skills); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/08/19/the-ai-job-that-surged-255-in-2026-and-pays-113000/)

#### Generative AI Engineer

**Evidence:** moderate. DataCamp has no dedicated guide, but its AI engineer guide treats the relevant skills as core.

**Mandate:** Builds reliable applications on foundation models. Postings for this role grew 197% year over year.

**Core skills:** prompt and context design; retrieval-augmented generation with vector databases; agentic systems built with frameworks such as LangChain and LangGraph; model evaluation; serving models as production APIs (for example with FastAPI); cloud deployment; responsible AI. Employer postings and courses outside DataCamp also list fine-tuning methods such as LoRA and QLoRA.

Sources: [16 Essential AI Engineer Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/essential-ai-engineer-skills); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rachelwells/2026/08/19/the-ai-job-that-surged-255-in-2026-and-pays-113000/); fine-tuning point: [Educative, Become an LLM Engineer](https://www.educative.io/path/become-an-llm-engineer)

#### Computer Vision Engineer

**Evidence:** moderate (DataCamp ML jobs guide and a webinar).

**Mandate:** Builds systems that interpret images and video, such as object detection, face recognition and pose estimation. DataCamp describes the role as highly specialized, with companies expecting novel or improved solutions in one domain.

**Core skills:** strong programming and software engineering principles; data structures and algorithms; system design; the mathematics behind machine learning (calculus, statistics, optimization, linear algebra); OpenCV; convolutional neural networks; PyTorch; Hugging Face. C++ is used where performance matters.

Sources: [The Best Machine Learning Jobs](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/top-machine-learning-jobs-in-2022); [16 Essential AI Engineer Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/essential-ai-engineer-skills); [AI for Visual Data webinar](https://www.datacamp.com/resources/webinars/ai-for-visual-data-computer-vision-in-business)

#### Python Developer

**Evidence:** moderate (DataCamp career track description). DataCamp's release groups this role with the infrastructure-oriented roles that showed strong growth.

**Core skills:** writing efficient Python and functions; manipulating data, including date, time, text and web data via APIs; unit testing with pytest; version control; packaging; NumPy. Typical uses are web development, data analysis and task automation.

Sources: [DataCamp Python Developer track](https://www.datacamp.com/tracks/python-programmer); [Business Wire release via 01net](https://www.01net.it/ai-drives-surge-in-technical-hiring-but-early-career-hiring-hit-hard-according-to-new-datacamp-report/)

## Adjacent roles

DataCamp's career guides document 14 further roles. Several match clusters in DataCamp's role selector (see the title map) and may be among its 25 main roles, but that is not confirmed, so they are kept separate from the profiles above.

**Modeling and AI**

- **MLOps Engineer.** Productionizes models built by data scientists: deployment, continuous monitoring, retraining, data and model versioning, and security and compliance. Skills: ML libraries (TensorFlow, Keras, PyTorch), ML fundamentals, software development, CI/CD pipelines.
- **Machine Learning Research Scientist.** Develops new algorithms and improves existing models; theory-heavy and specialized. Typically requires a master's or PhD, a record of research papers, a programming language, and depth in one ML area such as optimization.
- **Data Science Consultant.** Builds or designs AI solutions across many client industries. Skills match a data scientist's, with communication and presentation weighing more heavily; some firms expect both Python and R.
- **AI Product Manager.** Owns strategy for AI products from conception through launch. Skills: understanding of the machine learning lifecycle, product management practice, leading cross-functional teams, and handling ethical and regulatory issues.
- **Ethics in AI Specialist.** Addresses bias, fairness, transparency and accountability in AI systems. Skills: AI technology knowledge, ethical reasoning, strong communication.
- **Robotics Engineer.** Designs intelligent robotic systems. Skills: software development for robot behavior, hardware engineering, robotics principles.

**Data platform and governance**

- **Analytics Engineer.** Bridges data engineering and business intelligence, using tools such as dbt, Snowflake and Looker.
- **Data Governance Analyst.** Establishes and enforces policies so data is accurate, consistent and compliant. Skills: governance and data management experience, analytical problem-solving, communication, SQL, Python, Java.

**Analysts in a business function**

- **Data Visualization Engineer.** Designs and maintains interactive visualization systems and dashboards. Skills: Tableau, D3.js, R Shiny, Power BI, Python and R, communication.
- **Business Analyst.** Identifies business problems and designs solutions using diagnostic, predictive and prescriptive analytics. Skills: analytical and problem-solving ability, communication and presentation, project management, SQL, Excel, Tableau.
- **Marketing Analytics Manager.** Uses data to optimize campaigns and marketing strategy. Skills: SQL, Excel, Python, R, visualization and marketing analytics tools, communication.
- **Financial Analyst.** Analyzes financial data, builds models and forecasts. Skills: financial modeling, SQL, Excel, R, Python, communication.
- **Quantitative Analyst.** Applies mathematical and statistical methods to financial decisions such as risk, portfolio management and trading. Skills: Python, R, strong quantitative background.
- **Risk Analyst.** Identifies, assesses and mitigates organizational risk. Skills: risk analysis and management, Python, R, SQL, communication.

Sources: [The Best Machine Learning Jobs](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/top-machine-learning-jobs-in-2022); [7 AI Jobs You Can Pursue](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/jobs-in-ai-to-pursue); [The Top 10 Data Analytics Careers](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/top-ten-analytics-careers); [How to Become a Data Engineer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-data-engineer)

## Candidate roles with dedicated sources

These candidates from the title map have a DataCamp guide or enough sourcing to profile. Whether they are among DataCamp's 25 main roles is not confirmed.

#### Statistician

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide, updated August 2024).

**Mandate:** Collects and analyzes data using statistical modeling to solve business and research problems, and forecasts outcomes. Applied statisticians are the most common; theoretical statisticians work mainly in research and academia.

**Core skills, in order:** applied and theoretical statistical methods; mathematics (linear algebra, calculus); data mining and transformation; data visualization; computer science skills (machine learning, AI, programming, computing infrastructure). Tools: R is the most popular language, then Python, Java and Julia; specialized software such as SAS, SPSS and Matlab; databases such as SQL Server, PostgreSQL and NoSQL.

**Human skills:** communication, teamwork, curiosity.

**Specializations:** econometrician (economics) and biostatistician (biology and healthcare), with industry demand in research and academia, government, healthcare and sports. Most roles require a university STEM degree, and some a master's or PhD. Optional credentials include American Statistical Association accreditation, SAS and SPSS.

Source: [How to Become a Statistician](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-a-statistician)

#### Quantitative Analyst

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide, updated July 2024).

**Mandate:** Builds mathematical and statistical models to inform financial decisions in risk management, portfolio management and trading, including forecasting markets and building and testing trading algorithms.

**Core skills, in order:** calculus (derivatives, integrals, optimization); linear algebra; probability (random variables, distributions, hypothesis testing) and numerical methods; programming in R and Python; critical thinking; communication; attention to detail; knowledge of financial markets. Tools: R, Python, SQL, Excel.

**Typical education:** a bachelor's in mathematics, statistics, computer science or engineering, then often a master's in computational finance or financial engineering.

Source: [How to Become a Quantitative Analyst](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/how-to-become-quantitative-analyst)

#### Business Analyst

**Evidence:** strong (DataCamp guide, updated December 2024).

**Mandate:** Bridges business and analytics, defining requirements and implementing solutions rather than doing deep analysis or coding. DataCamp frames the role in four parts: decode (research and implement solutions), optimize (improve processes and troubleshoot analytical systems), document (requirements, scope and results) and communicate (translate data into actionable insight). The role is broader in small companies and narrower in large ones.

**Core skills, in order:** data analysis and visualization (SQL, Python, Power BI or Tableau); statistical and quantitative analysis; business intelligence tools; communication and presentation; problem-solving and critical thinking, including root cause analysis; interpersonal and negotiation skills; adaptability and continuous learning; machine learning and AI basics; emerging tools (generative AI assistants, AutoML, Looker Studio, collaborative platforms such as Databricks and Snowflake, and data privacy compliance tools).

Source: [The Top 9 Business Analyst Skills](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/top-business-analyst-skills)

#### Database Administrator

**Evidence:** thin. DataCamp's only published material found is an interview-question guide.

**Skills named:** database design principles, indexing and query optimization; database security, backup and recovery; SQL. Outside sources add performance monitoring, availability management and scripting for automation.

Sources: [DataCamp, Database Administrator interview questions](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/database-administrator-interview-questions); [Educative, What is a Database Administrator](https://www.educative.io/courses/database-administration-for-beginners/what-is-a-database-administrator)

#### NLP Engineer

**Evidence:** thin. DataCamp has NLP courses but no NLP engineer role guide.

**Skills named by outside sources:** Python; NLP libraries such as spaCy and NLTK; machine learning and deep learning; statistical analysis; linguistics; text preprocessing and feature extraction; communication.

Sources: [Coursera, NLP engineer](https://www.coursera.org/articles/nlp-engineer); [Coursera, essential NLP skills](https://www.coursera.org/articles/nlp-skills)

## How employers assess candidates

Across the guides, hiring combines evidence of what a candidate has built with technical tests matched to the role, and the portfolio often outweighs formal credentials. Forbes' summary of DataCamp's report says AI engineering employers look at computer science or ML education, formal or self-taught, but care more about what a candidate has built and the scale or impact it had.

**Interview stages by role family**

| Stage             | Data Engineer                                                                                   | BI and analytics roles                                             | ML, AI and computer vision roles                                                          |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Experience review | Past data work and projects, probed in detail                                                   | Portfolio walkthrough: problem, approach, tools, outcome           | What was built and its scale or impact                                                    |
| Coding            | A short timed problem in Python or Spark, such as a small pipeline that loads and cleans data   | Technical BI concepts, data analysis and visualization             | Data structures and algorithms questions (computer vision)                                |
| SQL               | Efficient queries over database data                                                            | Querying and data manipulation                                     | Not stated in the sources                                                                 |
| Design            | End-to-end data system design: storage, processing, modeling and the trade-offs between choices | Industry-specific knowledge of the employer's data needs           | System design for turning requirements into a product                                     |
| Fit               | Interview with prospective teammates                                                            | Behavioral questions on problem-solving, adaptability and teamwork | Fit for fast-paced teams; startups often prefer engineers from big tech or other startups |

**What a strong portfolio contains**

- **Range.** Several projects covering different skills, such as a pipeline, a data warehouse and a streaming job for data engineers, or cleaning, exploration, visualization and a dashboard for analysts.
- **One or two pillar projects.** In-depth projects that show the role's core skills, supported by smaller ones (BI guidance).
- **Real or realistic data.** Public datasets from Kaggle or government sources, tied to a business problem in a target industry.
- **Documentation.** For each project: the problem, the approach, tools, challenges and how they were resolved, published on GitHub.
- **Quantified impact.** Where possible, the decision or improvement the analysis would support.
- **Written explanation.** Blog posts or write-ups that show communication skills, the one competency every role requires.
- **Modern tooling.** Visible use of current tools (Airflow, dbt, Snowflake, Power BI, LangChain) signals industry relevance.

## Glossary

| Term                           | Meaning                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Agentic system                 | An AI application in which a model plans steps, calls tools and acts across several turns, rather than answering once           |
| CI/CD                          | Continuous integration and delivery: automatically testing every code change and deploying it once tests pass                   |
| Data contract                  | An agreed schema and set of quality rules between the team that produces data and the teams that consume it                     |
| Data lake                      | Low-cost storage for raw data in any format, structured or not                                                                  |
| Data lineage                   | A record of where data came from and every transformation it passed through                                                     |
| Data warehouse                 | A database optimized for analytical queries over cleaned, structured historical data, such as Snowflake, BigQuery or Redshift   |
| DAX                            | Data Analysis Expressions, the formula language for calculations in Power BI                                                    |
| dbt                            | A tool for writing, testing and documenting SQL transformations inside a warehouse                                              |
| ETL / ELT                      | Extract, transform, load: moving data from sources into storage. ELT loads first and transforms inside the warehouse            |
| Feature store                  | A shared repository of prepared model inputs, so training and production use the same values                                    |
| Fine-tuning                    | Further training a pretrained model on specific data; LoRA and QLoRA are lighter-weight methods                                 |
| Lakehouse                      | An architecture that adds warehouse-style tables and governance on top of a data lake                                           |
| Medallion architecture         | Organizing data in bronze (raw), silver (cleaned) and gold (business-ready) layers                                              |
| MLOps                          | Practices for deploying, monitoring, versioning and retraining machine learning models in production                            |
| Model drift                    | Loss of model accuracy as real-world data shifts away from the training data                                                    |
| Orchestration                  | Scheduling and coordinating pipeline steps and their dependencies, for example with Airflow                                     |
| Prompt and context engineering | Designing the instructions and supporting information given to a language model so its output is reliable                       |
| RAG                            | Retrieval-augmented generation: retrieving relevant documents and giving them to a model so its answer is grounded in that data |
| Star schema                    | A warehouse design with a central fact table of events linked to dimension tables that describe them                            |
| Stream processing              | Processing events continuously as they arrive, with tools such as Kafka, Flink or Spark Streaming                               |
| Vector database                | A database that stores embeddings and finds the most similar items quickly; used to power RAG                                   |
